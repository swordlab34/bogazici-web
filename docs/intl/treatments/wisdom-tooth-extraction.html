Treatments · Oral surgery

Wisdom tooth extraction

Content last reviewed: 1 November 2026 Medically reviewed · Dt. Mustafa Onur İmamoğlu

Should every wisdom tooth be removed?

No. Wisdom teeth that have come through properly, can be kept clean and cause no problems can stay in the mouth. The decision to extract depends on the tooth's position, the surrounding tissues and the problems it causes.

When is extraction recommended?

  • Repeated inflammation of the gum over the tooth
  • Decay or root damage in the neighbouring tooth
  • A cyst-like formation around an impacted tooth
  • A wisdom tooth that cannot be cleaned and keeps decaying

What is checked before the procedure?

The position of the tooth is assessed with a panoramic X-ray and, if needed, a three-dimensional scan (CBCT). The roots of lower wisdom teeth can lie close to the nerve that gives feeling to the lip and chin; this is taken into account when planning the procedure. Tell us at the examination about any medicines you take, especially blood thinners.

How is it done?

The extraction is done under local anaesthesia. For impacted or partly impacted teeth the gum is opened, the tooth is removed, in sections if necessary, and the area is closed with stitches.

After the extraction

Swelling, pain and limited mouth opening are possible in the first days. Take the medicines your dentist recommends, apply cold to the outside of the cheek on the first day, do not smoke and do not use a straw. Contact the clinic if bleeding does not stop, if you develop a rising fever or if swelling increases after a few days.
